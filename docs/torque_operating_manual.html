Torque — Operating Manual

What every control does and what every reading on screen means, in plain language. This is the user-facing guide — for how Torque is built, deployed, or configured, see docs/torque.md instead.

Read this first
Nothing on this page is a forecast. Torque shows you where premium currently sits and which way it's moving — deterministic math over the live options chain, never a prediction of where price will go. Every signal here is context for a trade you already had in mind.

1The controls — what each button does

Left to right, top to bottom, everything you can click or type into.

Ticker dropdown
Switches the underlying. First entry (NDX) is selected on load. Switching tickers resets Lock Strikes, the session-flow history, and Counter Read's spot-trend buffer — none of that carries over between tickers, since it's all tied to that ticker's own price scale.
Strategy grid
10 tiles, single-select: Long Call, Long Put, Bull Call, Bear Put, Bull Put, Bear Call, Iron Condor, Iron Fly, Call Fly, Put Fly. Click one to build it. On load (and on every ticker switch) the grid auto-lands once on whichever structure the current Positioning read makes playable — see "does the auto-pick keep changing?" below. Your manual pick always sticks afterward.
Lock Strikes toggle
Right of the spot line. Off (default): strikes continuously re-anchor to live spot as price moves. On: strikes freeze at whatever spot was the instant you toggled it — premiums keep updating live for those frozen strikes, only the strike selection itself stops drifting. The −/+ steppers still work, relative to the frozen anchor. Turn it back off to resume normal live-anchoring. Resets to off on any ticker or strategy change.
Leg steppers − / +
Next to each leg's strike. Nudges that one leg up/down the real strike grid (skips to the next actually-listed strike, never an invented one). Works whether or not Lock Strikes is on.
Net price card
Bid / Mid / Ask for the whole spread, debit or credit, summed directly off live chain quotes (never a separate quote call that could drop a leg). Refreshes every 3s along with the strikes. If any leg's quote goes briefly missing, the display holds its last good number rather than flash a broken partial price.
Limit Price
Pre-fills to the current mid; type any price to override. Feeds the Send Limit button.
Qty
Contracts, 1–50.
TIF
DAY or GTC (good-till-canceled).
auto-close +N% toggle + %
Arms a profit-target close the instant the entry fills — see Auto-close. Default 30%, editable; some tickers have a raised floor (DJX: 60% default, 45% minimum — its spread is too wide for the global default to ever fill). On tickers where auto-close is mandatory the toggle is locked on and marked required — you can still edit the % but can't disarm it.
SEND LIMIT button
Places the entry at your typed limit price (with the auto-close close armed behind it if the toggle is on).
SEND MARKET button
Places the entry at market. Disabled on tickers whose book is too wide for a market order to be safe (e.g. DJX — its ask has been measured at up to 4× its own mid).
DRY RUN button
Submits entry + close to the broker with preview=true — validates format, symbols, and buying power, places nothing. Use it before market open to catch format issues ahead of time. See Dry Run below for how to read the result.
Working / Past Orders tabs
Bottom panel. See The Orders panel.

2Positioning: the Lean

The big signed number (−100…+100) at the top of the Positioning panel. It's a deterministic blend of three things in the live chain (±3% around spot) — not a price forecast, just where the current options book already sits:

InputWhat it measuresWeight
Volume P/Ccall vs put contracts traded today40%
Premium skew (its own tile)call vs put $ resting in open interest30%
Magnet (its own tile)pull toward the most OI-concentrated strike near spot30%

Every label the Lean can show

ReadingLabelMeaning
≥ +25call-leaningbook sits call-heavy
≤ −25put-leaningbook sits put-heavy
−25…+25balancedno edge either way — deliberately not forced to a side

Think of the Lean as where the crowd already is — slow-moving, built from standing positioning, so on its own it can be stale (call-heavy because of yesterday, not because of anything happening right now). That's what the Flow (next section) is for.

3Premium skew & Magnet

Both tiles are labeled "Live read, no window" right above them — they're built from open interest × the current live mid, recomputed fresh every poll. That's a different kind of read from everything in the Flow/Session Flow sections below: those accumulate over an explicit 3-minute or 60-minute window, these two don't accumulate over any window at all. Hover that small label for the full explanation (why "no window," not "live OI" — OI itself is a standing count, not something that updates by the second the way mid/spot do).

Premium skew (tile)

Call vs put dollars resting in open interest right now — the standing balance, and one of the Lean's three inputs. Hover it on screen for a one-line reminder of its purpose. Shown as a label:

LabelMeaning
calls richmore $ resting on the call side
puts richmore $ resting on the put side
balancedno meaningful tilt

Magnet (raw OI) (tile)

The near-spot strike with the most concentrated open interest, plus its distance from spot (e.g. 29,430 · 0.7%). A tight near-spot magnet is what flips the auto-picked balanced strategy from Iron Condor to Iron Fly — Torque reads a strong pin as worth selling directly at, not around.

The tile is labeled "(raw OI)" on purpose, and hovering it explains why: this is plain open-interest concentration, with no gamma weighting — a much simpler read than a GEX-based dealer-hedging wall (the kind the main EdgeLane app computes). A strike can carry heavy OI but low gamma (stale, far-out positioning) or modest OI but high gamma (tight near-spot 0DTE concentration) — raw OI and GEX routinely point at different strikes. Magnet here is a rough context number feeding the non-predictive Lean, not a directional call the way a GEX wall is meant to be.

43-minute Flow

Reports the premium $ of new contracts traded on calls vs puts over the last 3 minutes — e.g. calls +$1.2M · puts +$0.4M · net +$0.8M call. Kept in this browser tab's memory only (clears on refresh), sampled every 5s. This is the fast tape — noisy on its own, but the earliest tell you get.

Why $0 on a side is meaningful
Each figure is new contracts in the window valued at current premium — not a mid-price diff, which would leak ordinary price movement into the number. Because volume only ever rises, +$0 on a side means literally zero new contracts traded there in the window — not "mids fell." A read like calls +$0 / puts +$1.5M under a call-leaning Lean is a genuine early warning, not noise.

Every flow-tag word

TagWhenMeaning
confirmsfresh money flows the same side as the Leanthe lean is live, has fuel
divergingfresh money flows the opposite side of the Leanthe lean is being faded, possible exhaustion
flatno decisive net flow (within ~6% of total)chop
call flow / put flowLean is balanced, so there's nothing yet to confirm/diverge fromjust names which side money is hitting — an early tell before the Lean itself tips
call flow / put flow often matures into confirms. On a balanced Lean, persistent put flow is usually the precursor: as it keeps up it drags the Lean itself down into put-leaning, and the exact same flow then reads as confirms. Watching that hand-off is an early read on a lean about to establish.

The key idea: the Lean tells you where the crowd is; the Flow tells you whether the crowd is still right. A small lean that flow confirms is often more tradeable than a big lean that flow is fading.

6Session Flow (the hour)

The 3m Flow is noisy tape; Session Flow integrates it over a trailing 60 minutes (cumulative delta in premium $) to answer: is there a persistent direction, or just chop?

What's on the tile

ElementMeaning
Net $Σ(call$ − put$) over the hour. A lone opposite print barely dents it — noise filters itself.
Signed bar — lengthmagnitude past the flip point (call/put parity), |net|/gross. Right/green = call side, left/red = put side. Longer = harder push right now.
Signed bar — colorcommitment, independent of length. Amber & pulsing = within ~10% of the flip line (contested, sign not trustworthy). Solid = persistent (≳70% of 3-min windows agree) and clear of the flip. Amber steady = building, in between.
X/Y windows put-side / call-sidehow many of the hour's 3-min windows agree on a side. This decides trend vs chop, not the raw %.
% call/put dominance|net|/gross as a live magnitude — how lopsided the current hourly imbalance is. Not an accumulation since the last flip.
held Nmtime since the hour's net last crossed zero (the flip). This is the only "since the flip" number — the % is not.
Length ≠ trust
A longer amber bar (hard push, not yet trusted) next to a shorter solid-red bar (milder but persistent) is normal and not a contradiction. Length answers "how hard, right now"; color answers "can I trust the direction." A short, committed drift is more tradeable than a long, contested lurch — don't read a bigger % as "closer to solid."

Persistence labels: ≳70% one side → trending put / trending call (a directional debit is in play; small counter-prints are pullbacks, not reversals). ~50/50, flipping → chop / range (Iron Condor/Fly turf). In between → building.

7Session bar × Lean — every combination

Session bar vs LeanSolid (persistent)Amber (contested/building)
Confirms (bar side = Lean) Strongest continuation — book and hourly flow agree. Ride the directional debit; a counter-print is a pullback. Flow agrees but isn't sticking → chop around an established lean → Condor/Fly.
Diverges (bar side ≠ Lean) ⚠ Early rotation — the book sits one way but persistent hourly flow is fading it. Flow leads, positioning lags — a regime change often starts here. Tentative fade — likely noise testing the lean. Wait for it to harden.

When the two agree by side, conviction is high; when they split, let the session bar's color break the tie. Always correlate by side, never raw numbers — the Lean is a −100…+100 composite, the bar % is a flow ratio; different scales.

8The ⚡ STRETCHED chip

A slim amber chip that appears in the Positioning panel only in one specific config: the session bar is trending solid one way, but the Lean is committed, skew is rich, and the freshest 3m flow have all turned the other way — the book-leads/hour-flow-lags case.

Reads ⚡ STRETCHED · {call/put} favored. Prior: this tension usually resolves by the hour-flow reverting toward positioning (the session bar is the side likely to give), not the book collapsing to meet the flow — both because the resting book is where dealers defend, and because the hour's residue decays as its oldest prints age out of the window. Hover it for the full read and its invalidation (skew capitulating is the tell that this is a real regime change instead). It's a state, not advice — see the cheat-sheet for the full writeup.

9Counter Read — the band on the order builder

Everything above reads the market. Counter Read reads the other side of your own trade — a live band next to the Limit/Qty/TIF/Send controls, showing whether premium is leaning toward the structure you're building or its natural opposite.

Why it's not redundant with put-call parity

A vertical and its mirror at identical strikes are arbitrage-locked — one side richening necessarily cheapens the other by the same amount, so they'd carry no independent information. Torque's counter side uses its own, independently-configured strikes (call-side and put-side offsets are set separately per ticker), so the two sides are priced off genuinely separate parts of the chain — a real, live skew read.

The band, piece by piece

The band works slightly differently depending on whether you're on a paired strategy or an Iron Condor/Fly, because only the pairs have a genuine "counter" to fix on:

Flips live to whichever wing currently has the pull — "Call legs" when calls are ahead, "Put legs" when puts are ahead, "balanced" when neither is.
Number next to the name that fixed counter's own share of the combined total — shown in every zone, including contested, since a fixed name's own share is meaningful either way. Always matches the fill bar's length exactly. whichever side is currently named its own share, recalculated to match whichever name is showing — so "Put legs 89%" always means puts are genuinely at 89%. Why the pair and self-split cases differ
On a pair, "Bull Call" is a separate play worth watching regardless of who's winning right now — so its name stays put. On Iron Condor/Fly there's no separate play, just two wings of the trade you're already building — so naming whichever one currently has the pull is more useful than fixing on one arbitrarily. An earlier version fixed the number to one side while letting a label imply the other (e.g. a tiny "1%" sitting glued to the pricier label) — that's what this redesign specifically fixes: the number shown always matches whatever name is bold above it.

Confirms / diverging — checked against real price, not Lean

Whichever side the band favors has its own structural bull/bear bias — not measured, just definitional (a bull put spread wins if price holds up, full stop). Torque compares that bias to the underlying's actual spot movement over the same ~20s window as the smoothing (a plain rolling buffer of live spot, with a small ~0.04% deadband so tick noise doesn't flip it):

TagMeaning
confirmsthe leaning side's bias matches where spot is actually moving
divergingthe leaning side's bias contradicts where spot is moving
(no tag)spot hasn't moved past the deadband in the last ~20s — nothing to check yet
Why spot, not Lean
Lean is itself 40% volume P/C + 30% premium skew + 30% OI-magnet — built entirely from the options chain, the same universe of data richness comes from. Checking richness against Lean would just be cross-referencing two flavors of the same premium/OI data, not validating against price. Spot is the one number on the page genuinely independent of all of it.

This is a relative-value lean, not a forecast — where premium is currently being bid and whether real price agrees, not which way price will go from here. A counter side that's richening, committed, and confirmed by price while you're about to send the other side is the "don't-enter-blind" case this exists for; use it to adjust strikes/distance, not as a standalone signal on its own.

The state and the price tag are independent — the tag is silently absent whenever spot hasn't moved enough recently, which is common and not an error. The complete set:

Seven shapes total. contested never carries a price tag or a held time — there's no lean yet to check, or time to have held one.

Retired wording
An earlier version of this feature warned "selling the richer side might be riskier" for credit pairs. That's gone — it only held when richness was driven by adverse price movement, and richness can just as easily rise because price is moving favorably for that side, in which case "riskier" was backwards. Confirms/diverging replaced it: a neutral read of agreement, never an instruction to avoid anything.

11Cross-tab verdict cheat-sheet

Reading everything above together. Each is a prior, not a promise — the named invalidation is what tells you the prior is breaking.

All tabs agree

Session bar, Lean, skew, and 3m flow all the same side → highest conviction; ride the directional debit; a small counter-print is a pullback. Invalidation: 3m flow flips and the session bar starts shrinking off its high.

Flow leads, book lags

The session bar has flipped solid to a new side, but the Lean is still balanced and skew still reads the old side → rotation in progress, not yet real. Wait for the Lean to cross ±25 and the tag to turn confirms. Invalidation: the session bar fades and re-flashes before the Lean crosses → it was a flow-only pop (fakeout).

Book leads, hour-flow lags

The Lean, skew, and freshest 3m flow are all one side, but the 60m session bar is still deep the other way (the case the ⚡ STRETCHED chip flags). The hour's flow is fighting the entire structure. Prior: this tends to resolve by flow reverting toward positioning — the session bar grinds back and flips — not by the book collapsing to meet the flow, both because the resting book is where dealers defend and because the hour's residue decays as it ages out of the window. Invalidation (a real regime change): skew capitulates, or fresh 3m flow swings back to the session-bar side.

Chop / contested

Session bar amber (contested near the flip), Lean balanced, 3m flow flat → no trusted side → premium-selling/range territory (Condor/Fly), not a directional debit. Invalidation: the bar hardens solid off the flip and windows push past 70% one way.

Prior, not law
The "flow reverts toward positioning" verdict is an intraday tendency, not a guarantee. On a true trend day positioning does collapse to meet the flow — that is a regime change — and the skew flip is your early warning you're in that case instead. Trade the prior, respect the invalidation.

12Auto-close & Dry Run

Auto-close

The profit target is a % return on the entry's debit/credit:

  • Debit spreads/singles (Bull Call, Bear Put, flys, Long Call/Put) — close = sell-to-close at 1.30× entry debit (default 30% target).
  • Credit spreads (Bull Put, Bear Call, IC, Iron Fly) — close = buy-to-close at 0.70× entry credit (keep 30% of the credit).

Single-leg limit entries use a native broker OTO bracket (the exit survives disconnects). Everything else — all spreads, any market entry — uses an app-managed background watcher: the entry is submitted and the request returns immediately, then a background task waits for the fill and places the close only once it's confirmed. If the entry is rejected, canceled, or never fills, no close is placed — a rejected/unfilled entry never leaves a naked close behind.

Dry Run

Submits entry and close to the broker with preview=true — validates format, symbols, and buying power without placing anything.

  • Entry → ✓ accepted, or the real reason it wouldn't be (e.g. insufficient buying power — an actual account constraint, not a format error).
  • Close → can't be fully previewed until a position exists to close, so the broker replies "cannot be placed unless closing a position." Torque recognizes this and reports format valid, validates live after fill — confirming the close is well-formed. A genuine format/symbol error surfaces differently, distinctly.

13The Orders panel

Working (default tab)

Only live orders — entries still resting plus GTC profit-target closes, newest-first. Each row's limit price is click-to-edit (Enter or ✓ submits a modify), and has a cancel button. An auto-close watcher strip shows an armed close still waiting on its entry to fill; once the close is actually placed it becomes a normal working order in the table. Polls every ~4s.

Past Orders

Account-wide, newest-first: every Torque-tagged order today at any status (pending → filled/canceled/rejected/expired). Identical from every tab, always reflects the broker's own live status. An order rejected at submission never becomes a broker order, so it won't appear here — the red "✗ ENTRY REJECTED" panel right after you send is your record of that.

The order-status confirmation under the builder ("✓ ENTRY SENT · entry id N") auto-dismisses after 10s — the order itself lives on in Working/Past Orders below.
