Yes, absolutely. Not only can this be built, but what you are describing is essentially the architecture of a modern quantitative sentiment-and-flow engine used by proprietary trading desks and advanced retail analytics platforms.

Because your end goal is specifically selling credit spreads to collect premium, the engine’s job isn't just predicting direction; it is predicting where price is unlikely to go and whether option volatility (premium) is overpriced.

Here is a breakdown of the exact data parameters and architecture you need to feed into this engine.

1. Sentiment & Catalyst NLP Engine (The "News & Noise" Module)
This module measures the narrative driver and checks if a sudden catalyst could ruin a credit spread.

Real-time News Sentiment Score (NLP): Natural Language Processing (using models like FinBERT or an LLM pipeline) parsing ticker news feeds to generate a continuous score from −1.0 (extreme panic) to +1.0 (extreme hype).

News Velocity & Spike Detector: Measures the volume of news headlines per hour. A sudden 300% spike in headline frequency—even before sentiment is fully scored—usually precedes immediate volatility.

Catalyst & Event Lockout Flag: Boolean parameter checking for upcoming binary events (Earnings releases, SEC filings, analyst days, major semiconductor conferences, or CPI/FOMC releases).

Credit Spread Rule: Never sell credit spreads inside an unpriced catalyst window unless IV rank is astronomically high.

2. Options Order Flow & Smart Money Module (The "Direction" Engine)
You cannot predict future volume directly, but you can read where volume is aggressively flowing right now to infer multi-day institutional positioning.

Net Premium Flow (Buyers vs. Sellers): Total dollar volume spent on calls vs. puts. Crucially, this must differentiate between orders filled at the Ask (aggressive buyers pushing price) vs. Bid (sellers writing options).

Sweep & Block Order Aggression: Captures large institutional multi-exchange orders ("sweeps"). A cluster of $500k+ OTM Call Sweeps indicates immediate directional bias for the next 24–72 hours.

Volume to Open Interest (Vol/OI) Ratio: Identifies whether high daily volume is new positioning (Vol > OI) or just old trades closing out (Vol < OI).

3. Structural Market Maker & Volatility Metrics (The "Credit Spread Edge")
This is the most crucial layer for selling credit spreads. It tells you where the price will stall or bounce, allowing you to pick safe short strikes.

Gamma Exposure (GEX) & Delta Exposure (DEX) Walls:

Zero Gamma Level: The inflection point between high volatility (trending) and low volatility (mean-reverting).

Call/Put Gamma Walls: The absolute price levels where market makers are forced to buy/sell shares to hedge. These act as natural ceilings and floors for the stock over the next 1–5 days—the exact spot to place your short strikes outside of.

Implied Volatility Rank (IVR) / IV Percentile: Compares current IV to its 52-week high/low.

Credit Spread Rule: Only sell credit spreads when IVR is high (e.g., above 40–50%). This ensures you are getting paid maximum premium for your risk.

Expected Move (1-SD & 2-SD Ranges): Derived from the current price of the At-The-Money (ATM) straddle for the upcoming expiration. Your engine should automatically place credit spread strikes outside the 1-Standard-Deviation expected move.

Volatility Skew & Term Structure: Measures whether front-week options are significantly more expensive than back-week options (Backwardation vs. Contango). High front-week skew means panic is priced in, creating ideal short-term credit spread selling conditions.

4. Technical Guardrails & Anchor Levels
Anchored VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price): VWAP anchored from major swing highs, swing lows, or earnings days. Institutional order algorithms execute around these anchors.

Dark Pool Print Concentrations: Aggregated price levels where massive off-exchange equity trades settle. These act as multi-day support/resistance magnets.

How the Engine Makes a Decision (Composite Scoring Example)
To put it all together, your watchdog script would calculate a Composite Confidence Score for a ticker (e.g., Micron - MU):

Sentiment Score: +0.75 (Strong positive AI memory demand headlines)

Order Flow: $2.5M net call sweeps at the Ask (Bullish)

Volatility Rank: IVR is at 62% (High premium available)

Structural Wall: Massive Put GEX Wall identified at $95.00; 1-SD Expected Move points to $94.50.

Engine Output:

"Bullish bias confirmed. High IV detected. Recommend Bull Put Credit Spread with Short Strike at or below $95.00 (below GEX Wall & Expected Move) for Friday's expiration. Estimated Win Probability: 86%."
