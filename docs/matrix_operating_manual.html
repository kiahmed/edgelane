Matrix

A dealer-positioning spread optimizer that publishes one pick at a time — and grades every one of them.

Redesigned · legacy discontinued

This is not the old single-file spread optimizer. edge_lane.html, built from spread_optimizer_v4_7_html.jsx, is discontinued — along with its manual Detect bias from Greeks button, its strategy / target-delta / wing-width knobs, and its three side-by-side candidate cards. Matrix now runs continuously off a backend poller.

Now at matrix.facades.trade

Matrix ships under the Facades brand at matrix.facades.trade. facades.trade replaces EdgeLane as the public name, across the product, the domain, and everything customer-facing.

Internal note — not public

The backend still calls the system edgelane throughout: repo, config keys, DuckDB tables, service names, Vercel projects. That naming is internal only and is deliberately not surfaced to users. Nothing public carries the old name.

01 — The product

What Matrix is

Matrix reads the options chain as a map of where dealers are forced to hedge, then tells you which multi-leg spread that map actually pays for — and what premium it would take to be worth trading.

Every poll, the engine pulls a live chain, aggregates dealer gamma exposure into walls, derives a directional bias from where spot sits relative to those walls, generates candidate spreads across eight structures, and ranks them by a single composite score built from expected value, structural health, liquidity, limit-order feasibility, and probability of profit. The highest-composite candidate is published as the engine pick.

All of that is deterministic math. The language model in the stack writes prose narrative only — it never scores, ranks, or picks. If a number appears on the page, Python or JavaScript produced it.

The part no other product here does

Three minutes after Matrix publishes a pick, it re-prices that exact spread and records whether it made money. Each pick is scored once — on how it ended up, not on how many times it was re-checked — and those results accumulate into a published win rate. When the record turns, Matrix stops showing the number and says so. See Self-evaluation.

  3. Sign in. The session persists per browser tab.

Connect a broker

Market data comes from the operator’s own feed — you don’t supply it. A broker connection is only for routing your own orders, and until you add one the push-to-broker button stays disabled.

Open the avatar menu (top right) → Broker connections → Add:

ProviderWhat it needsNotes
TradierBearer token, environment (sandbox or production), optional account idLeave the account id blank and it resolves from your token. Full multi-leg support.

Your token never comes back to the browser. It is encrypted at rest and decrypted only server-side, which is why Test runs as a backend probe of the provider’s profile endpoint and returns just {ok, env, account_id}. Several connections can coexist; exactly one is active, and that’s the one orders use.

Check two things before your first live order

That the connection’s environment matches what you intend — a sandbox connection fills against simulated quotes — and that the account carries enough option approval: level 3 for credit spreads, level 4 for iron condors. A rejected order usually means one of these, not a bad ticket.

Place an order

From the engine pick, the order dialog runs preview then submit: preview validates the exact ticket with your broker — margin, option level, strike spacing — and places nothing, so a rejection costs you nothing. Only after it passes does submit go live, on your own connection.

The order path never auto-retries a submit. A network failure there is surfaced rather than repeated, because a retry could double-place a filled order.

03 — Legacy → now

What the redesign changed

The legacy build was an operator's console: you set the inputs, pressed a button, and read three candidate cards it produced on demand. The redesign inverts that. The backend polls continuously and holds the opinion; the page is a readout of a running engine, not a calculator you drive.

Legacy — discontinued
  • Green Detect bias from Greeks button; nothing computed until you pressed it
  • You chose the strategy, target delta, and wing-width preference by hand
  • Three candidate cards side by side — Conservative, Balanced, Aggressive
  • One dominant GEX wall
  • Four manual refresh buttons, each costing provider calls
  • Tabs labelled Tickets (3) and Lookup
  • No record of whether any of it worked
  • Single HTML file, browser-transpiled, config baked at build time
Matrix — current
  • No detect button. The poller refreshes bias, chain, and candidates on a ~20 second cadence
  • The engine selects the structure. All eight strategies stay scored and visible; the bias stars the ones it recommends
  • One engine pick, promoted with its composite, verdict, structure, and metrics
  • Bilateral walls — put wall as magnet, call wall as resistance, each with its own strength
  • Auto-poll with a user-set interval; a status row shows staleness
  • Tabs labelled Engine pick and Lookup
  • Every pick graded three minutes later and scored once; a rolling win rate over recent picks and a regime-shift banner
  • Deployed frontend on matrix.facades.trade, sign-in gated, talking to a live FastAPI + DuckDB service
If you are coming from the old manual

The scoring math did not change — it was ported from the JSX engine to Python and is held to 56 parity tests, so every threshold below still means what it meant. What changed is who turns the knobs. Anywhere the old manual told you to press a button, the engine now does it on a timer.

Width, delta, and DTE scaling

The legacy UI exposed target delta and wing width as controls. They still exist as engine parameters — the optimizer hunts a short-strike delta around 0.25–0.30 for credit structures, and scales wing width off the expected move: tight 1.0×, balanced 1.5×, generous 2.5×. That base width then scales by time to expiry: 0.4× at 0 DTE, 1.0× at 1–7 DTE, 1.5× beyond a week. Configured server-side now, not clicked.

07 — Pricing your entry

Limit-order tiers

When the market is not pricing a spread the way you would want, this section answers a different question: what premium would make it worth doing, and which of those prices the market is already offering.

TierEdge targetHintTypical scenario
modest0.75% of widthoften fillsPatient limit, frequently catchable on normal intraday noise.
balanced1.50% of widthpatientAchievable on minor dislocations.
strong3.00% of widthon dislocationsReal liquidity events, IV crush, earnings unwinds only.

Each tier resolves to a limit price — sell at or above for credit, buy at or below for debit — and gets tagged ✓ met when the live mid already gives you that edge, ○ reachable when a GTC limit could catch it, or hidden entirely when the premium is mathematically impossible on that spread.

The headline, in plain English

For credit structures the rule is simple: the more credit you collect, the better the trade. Breakeven is the credit at which expected value is exactly zero. For debit structures it mirrors — the less you pay, the better.

HeadlineWhat to do
Already at +$X — beats every tierTake it at market. You are collecting more credit (or paying less debit) than even the strong tier asks.
Already at +$X — beats modest/balancedPaid better than fair, not as well as possible. Taking it now is fine; a slightly better limit is also fine.
Slight +$X — under the modest thresholdMathematically positive but thin. Market works; a small patient limit gets a meaningfully better fill.
Sub-fair by $X — limit onlyThe market pays less than the trade is worth. Taking it at market is a math-loser. It only works if the market comes to you.

Reality check — when to walk away

A sub-fair verdict is not automatically worth waiting for. Size the gap before parking a GTC order.

◎ Sub-fair by $39.19 — limit-only setup. Need the market to come to you.
breakeven (EV=0): $70.79 · live: $31.60

Read: the market pays $31.60 in credit; this condor needs $70.79 to break even on EV. That is a 2.2× jump in collected credit — a real volatility spike, not a normal Tuesday.

Gap as % of breakevenRealistic to wait?
< 10%Yes — normal intraday noise closes this within a session.
10 – 30%Maybe — needs a meaningful move. A day or two.
30 – 60%Long shot — needs a real catalyst. Weeks.
> 60%No — structurally mispriced for your side. Skip.

The math behind the tiers

credit spreads — sell premium
breakeven_credit    = (1 − POP) × width
target_credit(tier) = breakeven + (tier_pct × width) / wall_factor
place a limit SELL at target_credit or higher

debit spreads — pay premium
breakeven_debit     = POP × width
target_debit(tier)  = breakeven − (tier_pct × width) / wall_factor
place a limit BUY at target_debit or lower

The wall factor is folded in so the target already accounts for any wall penalty or boost. The modest tier feeds the composite's limit-feasibility component and doubles as the trade-ticket fallback price.

The discipline this enforces

Instead of taking what the market gives, you tell the market what you would accept. Most days you do not fill. The days you do, you have extracted real edge from a market that briefly mispriced your structure.

08 — Trade evolution

Lookup projections

A Black–Scholes projection grid for the current engine pick. Rows are hypothetical spot levels spanning roughly ±2 expected moves; columns walk wall-clock time in 15-minute steps, ET-session-aware, until expiry. Cells hold projected premium, colored by close-now P&L.

It is not a forecast. It answers “if this happens, here is what the trade looks like” — where the close-now breakeven band sits, how much theta an extra hour buys, whether a run to your short strike is deep enough to want a roll.

Hover decomposition

The tooltip is the point of the grid. It splits close-now P&L into what is actually paying you:

Spot $182.50 · +30m
premium +$1.42 · close-now P&L +$58/contract

When delta dominates a green cell, the trade is direction-dependent — a reversal gives it back fast. When theta dominates, it is time-dependent and sitting on it is the play. That distinction decides how you exit.

Pricing assumptions

  • European exercise — slight error on early-exercisable American single-name puts near dividends.
  • Sticky-strike implied volatility: each leg's IV stays attached to its strike as spot moves.
  • Constant 5% risk-free rate, no dividends.
  • The center cell is anchored to the live mid; everything else fans out by Black–Scholes sensitivity.
  • A leg missing IV falls back to intrinsic value, and the card says so in amber.
09 — Beyond gamma

VEX / TEX lenses

The bias engine is built on GEX — gamma exposure × open interest, the structural map of where dealers must hedge. Two adjacent lenses sit alongside it for the cases where gamma alone is not the whole story.

LensThe magnet it maps
GEX wallWhere dealers pin price — the structural magnet.
VEX wallWhere vol gets re-priced fastest — the IV-shock magnet.
TEX wallWhere theta bleed concentrates — the time-decay magnet.

They serve two genuinely different jobs on the two tabs. On the engine pick, the question is “is this trade worth doing?” — a static positioning answer that helps you pick. On Lookup, it is “how does this evolve over the next three hours?” — a dynamic flow answer that helps you decide when to bail or hold.

The three-lens divergence strip

Most days you never see it. The strip appears only when the three lenses disagree by more than five strikes:

⚠ lenses diverge · GEX $235 · VEX $232 (pull below) · TEX $228 (pull below)

When VEX and TEX pull below the GEX wall, price has a second gravity well your spread is not structured for — tighten the short strike toward the VEX/TEX side, cut size, or wait for the lenses to re-converge. Divergence pulling above is the mirror.

Cluster markers on the grid

MarkerWhereMeaning
●Spot-row labelThe nearest strike is in the top quartile of vega × OI. If price drifts here and IV moves, premium re-prices faster than the linear model shows — roughly 1.3×.
⌛Time-column headerThe column is inside the theta-burn window. Shown only for DTE ≤ 1, where decay is not roughly constant per day. Actual decay may run ~1.4× the estimate.
amber ringAny intersecting cellSits where vol or decay effects concentrate; the tooltip appends the multiplier hint.

These multipliers are rules of thumb, not corrections — flags to widen your error bars, not numbers to compute with. The rule of thumb for the pair: divergence is for picking the trade; clusters are for managing it. If you enter, set GTC and walk away, the strip is the higher-value signal. If you babysit positions, the grid pays off more.

10 — The differentiator

Self-evaluation

Matrix is the only product in the suite that keeps score on itself — and the only one that will refuse to publish its own win rate when it knows it is out of sync.

The earlier approach graded the bias direction: did spot move more than 0.05% the predicted way, three minutes later. That was blind to the actual recommended trade and printed a naked percentage even while the read was falling apart. The current metric grades the called-out spread itself, in real premium.

The one metric — no branches

Applied to the engine pick only, never the also-rans:

  1. At decision, every poll — the pick's legs, its entry mid net premium, and its type (credit or debit) are saved onto the decision row.
  2. At eval, ~3 minutes later — the same saved legs are re-priced from the current chain. The original legs, not whatever is on top now; the bias may have rotated since.
  3. Favorable delta — premium movement in the spread's profit direction. A debit wins as it expands: fav = mid_now − mid_entry. A credit wins as it decays toward zero: fav = mid_entry − mid_now.
  4. Friction band — half the current bid/ask width, so a quote bounce is never scored as a win.
  5. Result — win above the band, loss below it, neutral inside it.
  6. Scored once per pick — steps 1–5 repeat every poll for as long as the pick stands, which is what drives the live readout. The score takes only one result per pick: the last grade before the engine moved on. See One pick, one result.
Why this is the honest version

There is no fixed percentage threshold — any real mid improvement that clears fill friction counts. And because the pick is built from the bias, a winning spread means the bias direction paid off in the structure it actually recommended. Spot-difference grading is gone.

Cadence

A decision is born every poll (~15 s). The evaluator wakes every 30 s only to ask whether any decision has passed the 3-minute window ungraded, grades those once, and sleeps. Thirty seconds is the check interval, not the grading frequency; no individual decision is ever re-graded. A held pick does accumulate many graded decisions over its life — the score keeps only the last of them.

One pick, one result

A decision row is written every poll, so a pick that stands for 45 minutes leaves ~170 graded rows while one that dies in 30 seconds leaves two. Counting each row as a separate trade made the win rate a measure of how long a pick lingered rather than how often the engine was right — and picks linger when conditions are calm, so the number flattered itself exactly when it was least informative.

The unit of scoring is therefore a pick, not a poll:

  • What counts as one pick — an unbroken run of polls holding the same legs. If the engine returns to the same strikes after picking something else in between, that is a second pick, not a continuation.
  • When it is settled — the moment the engine switches away. Its result is the last grade it carried, i.e. how the idea ended up.
  • The live pick is not counted — an idea still on screen has no final result yet. It appears in the score only once it is superseded.
Before — scored per poll
  • One held pick re-checked 170 times counted as 170 trades
  • Half of every graded row came from ~7 of ~100 daily picks
  • “Last 20” was almost always the current pick's last five minutes
  • Three consecutive losses could be one trade red for ~48 s
Now — scored per pick
  • One pick contributes exactly one result, however long it stood
  • Every pick carries equal weight, calm session or fast one
  • “Last 20” is 20 real decisions — roughly the past hour
  • Three consecutive losses means three picks that went wrong
Expect a lower, truer number

This is not a cosmetic change. On real history the same session read 70% per poll and 15% per pick — the old figure was one favourable position sampled repeatedly. A falling percentage here is the metric getting honest, not the engine getting worse. Higher is not the goal; knowing is.

Nothing about grading itself changed — same legs, same repricing, same friction band, same three-minute window. Re-checking the held pick every poll still happens and still drives the live position readout. Only what the score counts has changed, so the whole existing history re-reads under the new definition with no migration.

Bias-trust state

Grades roll into a single state that governs what the page is allowed to show. Precedence runs paused → low confidence → calibrating → in sync. Regime evaluation keeps running in every state — only the display changes.

Calibrating
Calibrating — 6/10 graded

Fewer than ten settled picks. No percentage published; the pick is tagged confirming edge — sizing in. Early in a session this is the expected state — picks accrue at roughly 8–16 an hour, so the bar is cleared by mid-morning rather than in the first few minutes.

In sync
64% win rate (37 graded)

Enough samples, no regime alert. The win rate is shown because it has been earned. No hint on the pick card. 37 graded means 37 settled picks — at roughly 8–16 picks an hour, a few hours of trading.

Out of sync — paused
⏸ recalibrating — bias re-syncing (2 confirming wins to resume)

Three consecutive losing picks tripped the regime alert — three separate ideas that went wrong, not one trade wobbling. The percentage is withheld until two confirming wins clear it. Grading continues silently underneath — those very evals are what clear the alert.

Low confidence
58% win rate (22 graded) — lower-conviction read

The bias card itself reads low, independent of any streak. The number still shows, muted, with a confirm-before-sizing-up hint.

The archive underneath

Raw decisions and outcomes are never flushed — they are the only labeled ground-truth dataset the system will ever have. Live reads are scoped instead (regime looks at today; accuracy at a rolling window of settled picks), and each completed ET day is rolled up once into a daily summary: counts, win rate, session span, largest polling gap, coverage.

Each day carries a complete flag — true only when the day covered at least 75% of the 390-minute session with no gap over 15 minutes. Partial days are kept but flagged, so modeling can require full sessions rather than silently averaging in a morning when the backend was off.

What the archive is for

  • Threshold tuning — is three losses / two wins actually the right regime gate? Replay real streaks and find out. Same for the friction band.
  • Confidence calibration — does a low bias reading genuinely lose more than high? Today that is an assumption. The data can prove it or kill it.
  • Per-strategy edge — does an iron condor in a low-conviction regime actually win? Strategy is persisted per decision, so this is a join away. Offline only: the live streak is deliberately strategy-agnostic, because it is a bias-trust signal, not a per-structure tracker.
  • Engine drift — is accuracy trending down week over week? Chart the daily summaries across complete days and watch for the model going stale.
11 — In practice

Workflow

The redesign removed most of the old steps. What remains is reading, judging, and pricing.

  1. Check the accuracy pill first. If it reads recalibrating, the engine is telling you its read is out of sync. Size accordingly before you look at anything else.
  2. Read the bias card — label, score, confidence. Low confidence means the signals do not agree; fade or reduce.
  3. Read the walls. Where is the magnet, where is the resistance, and how strong is each? Weak walls mean the wall-aware logic underneath is not load-bearing.
  4. Look at the engine pick's composite. Under 40, skip the setup entirely — there is nothing to salvage below that line.
  5. Check the health and liquidity badges. A capital trap or a low-OI multi-leg is a no regardless of composite.
  6. Price the entry from the limit tiers. Green at market, take it. Sub-fair, apply the gap triage before parking a GTC order.
  7. Optional — open Lookup. See what the spread looks like in thirty minutes, or if spot runs to your short strike. Check whether your exit horizon lands on a theta-cluster column.
  8. Push to broker from the pick card, or copy the ticket and place it yourself.
  9. Leave it open. The poller keeps updating; the regime banner will tell you if the read deteriorates. You no longer refresh anything by hand.
12 — The suite

Matrix vs. the others

Three products share one backend and one data budget. They do not overlap.

Matrix
